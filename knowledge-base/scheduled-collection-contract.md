# Scheduled market collection contract

Status: **architecture proposal for implementation review, not an activated schedule**. The roughly hourly whole-day refresh, bounded after-close rechecks, next-day correction, one workflow per refresh tick, and manual catalog publication are accepted behaviors from [workflows.md](workflows.md). The exact tick offsets, rate budget, and alert thresholds below are proposed starting values. No schedule should be registered or provider called merely because this document exists.

## Control boundary

Temporal may start collection work; it cannot decide that a held catalog is published or that an unreviewed ticker mapping is safe. A scheduled run reads application PostgreSQL in an activity, pins one `catalog_current` snapshot and one reviewed ongoing-collection approval for that snapshot, and then dispatches bounded five-stock activities. It never uses the isolated `alpaca-sample-v1` snapshot or the fictional fixture. The application database remains the source of catalog, calendar, aliases, bar revisions, and selected observations; Temporal retains small run IDs and outcomes.

The schedule action is a **candidate tick**, not permission to call Alpaca. A worker-side admission activity must pass every gate below before any provider request, including when an operator manually triggers a paused Temporal Schedule. Temporal permits manual triggers while a Schedule is paused, so the gate cannot live only in schedule registration. [Temporal Schedule pause behavior](https://docs.temporal.io/schedule).

| Gate | Required check at execution time | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runtime and rights | Production mode, explicitly enabled collection, reviewed account entitlement and private-use rights for the chosen feed. | No provider call; hold/alert. |
| Catalog | `catalog_current` points to a published `fmp-sp500` snapshot with at least 450 distinct members. Its snapshot ID equals the ongoing approval's snapshot ID. | No fallback to a sample or prior snapshot; hold/alert until the new catalog and mappings are reviewed. |
| Ongoing approval and mappings | An approved full-catalog mapping set covers every current stock; its identity check is current, and the active source/feed/basis is exactly the approved tuple. Approval stays active while these conditions and account rights remain valid; elapsed days alone do not end it. | No price request; hold/alert on missing, changed, or conflicting identity, source/feed/basis, or rights. |
| Calendar | The reviewed `US_EQUITY` calendar covers the target date, and stored UTC open/close and source match the approved calendar. Date and due slot come from this calendar, not weekday assumptions. | Skip a confirmed non-session; hold/alert if calendar coverage is absent or records conflict. |
| Capacity | A single account-wide request budget covers refresh, reconciliation, repair, and backfill, including pages and retries. A session pass has capacity ahead of historical backfill. | Defer/skip backfill first; report an unserved session slot rather than exceeding the budget. |

The current `validate_collection_group` checks the preparation ID, pinned snapshot/source/feed/basis, approved aliases, date bounds, and stored calendar before a five-stock fetch. The content checksum is checked at approval, not by this group validator. Its `requested_end` remains a hard bound for **historical backfill**: a five-year backfill preparation ending today cannot authorize tomorrow's refresh. Implement a separate scheduled-collection validation path, or extend the model with an explicit ongoing mode, that checks the current approval and target-date calendar without treating the historical backfill end date as a collection expiry. Do not bypass alias or calendar validation to achieve this.

Before scheduling, add a reviewed **ongoing-collection approval** tied to the published full-catalog snapshot, approved Alpaca asset IDs/aliases, source/feed/basis, and account rights. It has no routine date expiry or manual renewal. Admission checks the current published snapshot and approval on every pass, plus the target session's reviewed calendar coverage. A newly held FMP stage remains held until separate catalog review publishes it; publication of a different snapshot holds collection until its mappings are reviewed and approved. Source/feed/basis or rights changes also hold collection for review. This is an operating approval, not permission to publish a held catalog.

Before the first price pass on each New York date, a bounded asset-reference check compares the current Alpaca asset ID, symbol, and exchange with every reviewed mapping. Charge this request to the same provider budget and hold price collection if any identity drifts or the check fails. Later passes require that day's successful check and a still-current approved mapping set. This detects changes but does not prove historical identity.

## Proposed timing and idempotency

Register separate refresh and next-day reconciliation Temporal Schedules **paused initially**. The following offsets are proposed for a first controlled rollout and must be checked against full-universe provider behavior and rate measurements.

| Candidate schedule | Admission rule and business slots | Temporal policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refresh gate | A 30-minute UTC interval only proposes a tick. For a stored session, fetch the cumulative `1Day` bar at `open_at + 60 min`, every 60 minutes thereafter while before `close_at`, then at `close_at + 30`, `+90`, and `+180 min`. A gate accepts a slot only from its due time until less than 30 minutes later. This yields about six regular-session and three after-close passes on a normal 09:30–16:00 New York day, and naturally fewer on a half day. | `Skip` overlap and a 10-minute catchup window. A late or overlapping tick is recorded as missed; the next timely slot reads the latest whole-day bar. Do not replay every stale intraday tick after an outage. |
| Next-day reconciliation gate | At 08:00 `America/New_York` on every calendar day, check whether **yesterday's New York date** was a stored trading session. If so, refetch that one prior session as historical, then run the SQL completeness check and report corrections, omissions, and rejections. Weekend/holiday ticks with no preceding-day session make no provider call. | `Skip` overlap and a bounded two-hour catchup window. A missed daily run becomes an operator-visible repair item, not an unnoticed success. |

The exchange calendar's UTC open/close values determine slots across daylight-saving changes and early closes. Only the reconciliation trigger uses an IANA local time; 08:00 avoids the ambiguous DST transition hour. Temporal supports calendar time zones, overlap policies, and bounded catchup windows; its default catchup window is much longer than is appropriate for a stale provisional tick. [Temporal Schedule specification and policies](https://docs.temporal.io/schedule), [Python SDK policy fields](https://python.temporal.io/temporalio.client.SchedulePolicy.html).

Each admitted pass uses a stable business key: `{kind}:{session_date}:{slot_or_reconcile}:{snapshot_id}:{source}:{feed}:{basis}`. A duplicate scheduled or manual start for the same key must not fetch twice concurrently. Use a deterministic workflow ID or a durable uniqueness claim for that key; do not rely solely on the Schedule's overlap policy, which covers only executions started by that Schedule. Repeated page writes remain idempotent, and a later changed bar becomes a new accepted revision. A missed slot is not a saved market-data gap table; it is an operational missed-run signal. The final after-close observation stays provisional until a later historical recheck; none of these offsets proves provider finality.

For Alpaca Basic historical SIP access, the existing worker uses the historical multi-symbol `1Day` endpoint with a cutoff at least 15 minutes behind fetch time. It must keep the request's `feed` and `adjustment` pinned and follow `next_page_token` for every page. Do not silently substitute a latest snapshot or IEX bar. Alpaca documents a 15-minute historical-data restriction on Basic, a 10,000-point **total** page limit, and a published 200 historical calls/minute Basic allowance; these are provider documentation, not proof of this account's safe sustained budget or display rights. [Alpaca market-data limits](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api), [historical bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars), [market-data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.1/docs/market-data-faq).

## Rate and failure control

A full pass over roughly 500 stocks needs **at least** 100 five-stock requests before pagination or retries. The proposed six intraday and three after-close passes plus one next-day recheck imply about 1,000 minimum requests across those runs, spread through the day. This is a sizing example, not an approved quota. Admit a pass only with a measured account budget, bounded in-flight groups, enough time before the next slot, and reserved capacity for session work. `SessionRefreshWorkflow` now bounds in-flight groups with a semaphore (default two); the current one-second `_pace_provider` is still process-local, so neither supplies a distributed account-wide budget or proven priority scheme. Start with one collection worker process and pause or deprioritize backfill around session passes. A second provider worker requires a shared limiter first.

Transient timeout, 5xx, and 429 failures receive bounded activity retries; honor `Retry-After`. Auth, entitlement, malformed request, inactive or incompatible approval, alias conflict, or calendar conflict are non-retryable until reviewed. A failed group or incomplete returned-symbol count makes the pass `incomplete` or `failed` with counts and affected groups; a Temporal `Completed` workflow with business status `incomplete` must still alert. Do not infer success from workflow completion alone. Session refresh reports freshness/returned counts, not historical completeness; next-day reconciliation queries expected stock/session pairs from PostgreSQL and may request a bounded targeted repair or leave an explicit manual repair item. A later lookback for older corrections is still an operating design, not an enabled schedule.

Monitor Schedule action lag and missed catchup, workflow status **and business outcome**, provider 429s, request-budget use, oldest fetched timestamp, expected versus returned stocks, rejected bars, missing stored pairs, catalog/approval mismatch, and calendar coverage. Pause on entitlement or identity uncertainty. An outage does not automatically backfill missed intraday snapshots; the next valid refresh obtains the current cumulative bar. A missed next-day recheck receives a deliberate, bounded `PriceRepairWorkflow` after operator review. Temporal exposes Schedule missed-catchup and related metrics. [Temporal service metrics](https://docs.temporal.io/references/service-metrics).

## Implementation gates before unpausing

1. Confirm the FMP full catalog, historical alias review, reviewed ongoing mappings, calendar, Alpaca account rights, and source/feed/basis. Test catalog rollover, mapping drift, rights change, and missing calendar coverage; none can cause fallback to test data or automatic publication. Verify ordinary calendar progression continues without a manual approval renewal.
2. Implement the calendar/admission activity, stable pass key, account-wide request budget and session priority, and explicit incomplete/missed-run alert. Verify the existing bounded refresh fan-out at full-catalog scale. Keep provider pages and credentials out of Temporal history.
3. Verify ordinary, half-day, holiday, DST, after-close, overlapping, outage/catchup, 429, changed identity or catalog, missing calendar coverage, and duplicate manual-trigger cases in a deterministic local test clock. Measure a full-catalog pass and next-day recheck within the host and provider budget.
4. Review Schedule definitions and IDs, register them **paused** through an operator action, inspect their next action times and worker pollers, then unpause only after a separate release approval. No schedule registration or live provider call is part of this proposal.

Open operating choices: full-universe account budget and in-flight count, freshness alert thresholds, whether two or three after-close checks are justified by observed revisions, and the older-session lookback cadence. Price adjustment and dividend return policy remain separate unresolved analytical decisions.
